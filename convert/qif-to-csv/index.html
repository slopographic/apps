QIF to CSV converter

QIF is the old Quicken format that many banks and money programs still export. Turn it into a CSV you can open anywhere.

Accepts

QIF files (.qif). Other file types are not converted on this page.

How to convert

  1. Drop the .qif file. Bank, Cash, CCard and other asset or liability sections are read; category and investment sections are skipped.
  2. Each transaction becomes one row: date, value date, amount (money in positive, money out negative), currency, text, counterparty, reference, type, status and the bank’s id.
  3. QIF dates have no fixed order. Month first is assumed, as Quicken writes them, unless a date in the file has a first number above 12; then day first is used.

Your files stay on this device. Nothing is uploaded: the conversion runs in your browser, and the file is never sent to Slopographic or to any other server.

Questions

What about split transactions?

A split transaction stays one row with its total amount. The category of the whole transaction is kept.

Why is the currency column empty?

QIF files do not say which currency they are in, so the column is left empty.

Will Excel read the amounts as numbers?

Choose comma for an English Excel. For Excel in Swedish, German or another language with a decimal comma, choose semicolon: the amounts are then written 1234,50. The file starts with a BOM so letters such as å, ä and ö show correctly.
